### Why the base game requirement matters

The requirement is not a technicality. Cronos: Lazarus installs alongside Cronos: The New Dawn, and the store page will not let you buy or launch the expansion without the base game attached to your account. Practically, that means:

1. Buy or verify Cronos: The New Dawn first.
2. Confirm the base game is installed on the same drive you plan to use.
3. Launch the base game once so your account and save data are registered locally.
4. Then purchase and install the expansion on release day.

If you are new to the series, play the base game before the expansion. The story picks up inside a world you are expected to recognize, and the protagonist is a character the base game already established.

## What the Lazarus DLC Changes: Combat, Story, and Tone

Platform questions are only half the picture. The other half is what you are actually installing, and the expansion shifts the formula in a direction the base game deliberately avoided.

You play as the once-loyal Pathfinder, mid-transformation into the Warden fans of the base game already know. Isolated in the Terminal and cut off from the Collective, he spends every waking hour trying to awaken a lost Essence he refuses to let fade. His sanctuary, however, has been compromised, and a new kind of hunter has been deployed with a single directive: find him and eliminate him.

| Element | Cronos: The New Dawn | Cronos: Lazarus |
|---|---|---|
| Playable perspective | The Warden as established | The Pathfinder becoming the Warden |
| Combat philosophy | Precision; picking your battles | Faster, more aggressive engagements |
| Signature tools | Base-game kit | Decoy, temporary invisibility, enhanced traversal |
| Pressure source | Survival in the wastelands | A hunter engineered specifically to kill you |
| Encounter scale | Measured | Large-scale, cinematic set pieces |

The ability list is where the tone shift becomes concrete. Leaving a decoy in your wake and briefly vanishing from enemy perception are tools reserved for a Pathfinder, and they reward players who plan an escape route before a fight rather than during one. Enhanced traversal compounds that: the expansion expects you to keep moving, reposition, and break line of sight instead of hunkering down behind cover.

The antagonist design reinforces the same idea. The new pursuer is described as relentless, highly adaptive, and strong enough to match the Warden directly — which means brute force alone is a losing strategy. Community discussion around similar pursuit-style enemies in action games tends to converge on one lesson: control the arena before the fight starts. Given the tools on offer here, that advice looks likely to hold.

Narratively, the expansion is framed around fragments of the Warden's past, a revival mission pursued at any cost, and a choice about how far you will go to defy the Collective. If you care about the lore of New Dawn, this is where the connective tissue lives.
