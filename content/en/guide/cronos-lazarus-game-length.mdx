## What Will Actually Shape Your Cronos Lazarus Game Length

Two players will finish this expansion hours apart, and it will rarely be because one is "better." Playtime is a product of choices, and these are the levers that move it most.

| Factor | Shortens Your Run | Lengthens Your Run |

| Combat approach | Aggressive play using Warden tools | Cautious, cover-heavy engagements |

Since the expansion is built around a more aggressive power fantasy, the fastest players will be the ones who commit to pressure rather than the ones who play it like the base game.

## Tips to Control Your Own Cronos Lazarus Game Length

Whether you want a tight four-hour sprint or a leisurely eleven-hour sweep, you can steer the outcome. These are the highest-leverage adjustments.

- **Pick your difficulty on purpose.** If you want to experience the story once and move on, do not default to the hardest setting out of pride. The pursuer fights are where difficulty converts most directly into hours.
- **Learn the decoy early.** The moment you unlock the decoy, practice it in a low-stakes room. Players who internalize it early report far fewer forced fights — a community pattern worth watching for post-launch.
- **Do not hoard invisibility.** Temporary invisibility is a traversal tool as much as an escape button. Using it to skip a lethal room is usually worth more than saving it "just in case."
- **Set a collectible rule before you start.** Decide up front whether you are chasing every fragment of the Warden's past or only the ones on your path. Ambiguous intentions are how a five-hour run becomes a nine-hour one.
- **Skip the completionist guilt on run one.** A focused first pass, then a deliberate cleanup run, is almost always faster than trying to do everything while also learning the layout.
- **Check performance settings.** Minimum specs call for 16 GB of RAM and a GTX 1080-class card. Stable frame rate during cinematic encounters is not a luxury — it is the difference between a clean dodge and a death loop.
