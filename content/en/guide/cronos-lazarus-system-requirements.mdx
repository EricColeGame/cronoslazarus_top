The Lazarus Protocol unlocks on December 3, 2026, and if you plan to return to the wastelands of New Dawn as the Warden, the first thing worth checking is whether your rig can actually handle it. The **Cronos Lazarus system requirements** look modest on paper, but they hide a couple of gotchas — especially on the graphics card side. Below you'll find the full official **Cronos Lazarus system requirements** list, a plain-English breakdown of what each component actually means, and practical upgrade advice so you aren't scrambling on launch day.

## Cronos Lazarus System Requirements at a Glance

Cronos: Lazarus is a paid expansion for Cronos: The New Dawn, and its Steam listing currently publishes a single minimum spec tier. Here is the full official list, exactly as posted:

| Component | Minimum Requirement |
|---|---|
| **OS** | Windows 10 x64 |
| **Processor** | Intel Core i5-8400F, Intel Core i5-8600K, or AMD Ryzen 5 3600 |
| **Memory** | 16 GB RAM |
| **Graphics** | NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1080, AMD Radeon RX 5700-XT, or Intel Arc A770 |
| **DirectX** | Version 12 |
| **Storage** | 30 GB available space |
| **Sound Card** | Windows-compatible audio device |

A few things jump out immediately. First, there is no "Recommended" tier listed yet — only minimums. Second, the minimum GPU is unusually high for a minimum. A GTX 1080 was a flagship card in 2016, and asking for one as the floor means the DLC is targeting a fairly modern machine even at the lowest settings.

Also worth noting: this is a downloadable expansion, so you need **Cronos: The New Dawn** installed to play it. The 30 GB figure covers the DLC's own footprint, not the base game.

## Breaking Down Each Spec in Plain English

Knowing the Cronos Lazarus system requirements is only half the job — the useful part is understanding what each line item is really telling you about your machine.

### Processor: Six Cores Is the Floor

All three listed CPUs are six-core chips from the 2017–2019 era. The Ryzen 5 3600 has SMT (12 threads), while both Intel options are six-core, six-thread parts. The practical takeaway: a modern six-core processor is the baseline, and older quad-core gaming CPUs are the most likely bottleneck.

| Listed CPU | Cores / Threads | Launch Era |
|---|---|---|
| Intel Core i5-8400F | 6 / 6 | 2018 |
| Intel Core i5-8600K | 6 / 6 | 2017 |
| AMD Ryzen 5 3600 | 6 / 12 | 2019 |

If you're running a Ryzen 5 2600, an i5-7600K, or anything older, expect to feel it — the Warden's faster, more aggressive combat style means more enemies on screen and more physics-driven encounters than the base game's more deliberate pacing.

### Memory: 16 GB Is Now the Entry Point

Sixteen gigabytes is listed as the minimum, not the recommendation. That's a meaningful shift from a few years ago, when 16 GB was the comfortable target and 8 GB was the floor. If you're still on 8 GB, this is the cheapest and most impactful upgrade you can make before December.

### Graphics: The Real Gatekeeper

The three listed GPUs sit in a similar performance class, and all ship with at least 8 GB of VRAM in their standard configurations:

| Listed GPU | Architecture | Typical VRAM | What It Signals |
|---|---|---|---|
| GeForce GTX 1080 | Pascal (2016) | 8 GB | Older flagship still punching above its weight |
| Radeon RX 5700-XT | RDNA 1 (2019) | 8 GB | Solid 1440p-class card from the last console generation |
| Intel Arc A770 | Alchemist (2022) | 8 GB or 16 GB | Modern budget-to-midrange option with strong DX12 support |

Two things follow from this table. First, 8 GB of VRAM is effectively the floor — cards with 4 GB or 6 GB are going to struggle regardless of raw compute. Second, the Arc A770 appearing on an official minimum list is a good sign that the developers are testing DX12 Ultimate features properly, not just legacy NVIDIA hardware.

### OS, DirectX, and Storage

Windows 10 x64 is the stated OS. Windows 11 runs the same DirectX 12 feature set and is almost certainly fine, though the store page doesn't say so explicitly. The DirectX 12 requirement means you'll want current GPU drivers — especially on Arc, where driver maturity matters far more than on GeForce or Radeon.

The 30 GB storage figure is for the DLC alone. Add your base game install on top of that, and budget a comfortable buffer for shader compilation caches and patches.

## Pre-Launch Setup Checklist

Before December 3, work through this list so launch day is smooth:

- **Update your GPU driver** to the latest Game Ready or Adrenalin release. Arc owners should be especially diligent.
- **Verify free space** — 30 GB for the DLC plus base game plus a patch buffer.
- **Confirm your RAM is running at rated speed** by enabling XMP or EXPO in BIOS. Many 16 GB systems run underclocked by default.
- **Check your PSU headroom** if you're upgrading a GPU to meet the minimum.
- **Wishlist the DLC** so you get a notification the moment it unlocks.
- **Verify base game ownership** — the expansion requires Cronos: The New Dawn.

If you're building fresh around these specs, prioritize the GPU and 16 GB of RAM first, then a modern six-core CPU. That order matches how the Cronos Lazarus system requirements are weighted.

## FAQ

**What are the Cronos Lazarus system requirements?**

The official minimum asks for Windows 10 x64, an Intel Core i5-8400F, i5-8600K, or AMD Ryzen 5 3600, 16 GB of RAM, a GeForce GTX 1080, Radeon RX 5700-XT, or Intel Arc A770, DirectX 12, and 30 GB of free storage.

**Can I run Cronos Lazarus with 8 GB of RAM?**

Not according to the published specs. Sixteen gigabytes is listed as the minimum, and memory is one of the cheapest upgrades you can make, so it's worth addressing before launch rather than after.

**Do I need to own Cronos: The New Dawn to play the DLC?**

Yes. Lazarus is an expansion, and the store page states it requires the base game on Steam in order to play. Budget storage space for both.

**Are there recommended specs for Cronos Lazarus yet?**

No. Only the minimum tier has been published so far. Recommended specs, target frame rates, and upscaling support haven't been confirmed, so check the Steam listing again closer to the December 3, 2026 release date.

**Will Cronos Lazarus run on a gaming laptop?**

It depends on the GPU. A laptop with a full-power RTX 3060-class chip and 16 GB of RAM should clear the minimum bar, while thin-and-light models with 4 GB or 6 GB of VRAM likely won't. Compare your laptop GPU against the desktop cards in the table above, and remember laptop versions are usually a tier or two slower than their desktop namesakes.
